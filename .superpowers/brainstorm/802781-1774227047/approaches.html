Three Navigation Approaches

The core problem: visitors think "Cascade County" but the site is organized by content type. Which approach fixes this best?

A

Geography-First Nav

Replace the type-based top nav with a county selector as the primary entry point. Every page stays anchored to a county context. "Pick your county → see everything for it."

Pros

  • Matches how visitors think
  • One click to your local data
  • Works for all 56 counties

Cons

  • Biggest nav restructure
  • First-time visitors still need to pick a county
B

Homepage County Hub

Keep current nav but redesign the homepage as a county-picker landing page: prominent search bar + browsable county cards. Once you pick, a sticky "county context" bar follows you through the site.

Pros

  • Low risk — nav stays the same
  • Immediate orientation on arrival
  • Sticky context prevents getting lost

Cons

  • Doesn't fix nav for direct/deep links
  • Two UX paradigms in parallel
C

Grouped Mega-Nav + County Breadcrumb

Reorganize the top nav into grouped dropdowns (Records, Courts, Community, Resources) and add a county breadcrumb/switcher that appears on every data page. Best of both worlds.

Pros

  • Scales to all content types
  • Fixes both "what is this site?" and "where am I?"
  • Works on mobile as a drawer

Cons

  • Most moving pieces
  • Dropdown UX can be finicky on mobile

My recommendation

Start with B — it solves the immediate problem (visitors arriving at the homepage lost) with the least risk. Then layer in elements of C over time as the site grows. A is the eventual ideal but requires the most restructuring.
